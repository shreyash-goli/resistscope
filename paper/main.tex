\begin{abstract}
Antiviral drug resistance forces medicinal chemists to triage candidate inhibitors by how well their binding survives known resistance mutations. We created \textbf{ResistScope}, a target-agnostic pipeline that docks a candidate against wildtype plus a panel of clinical resistance mutants, scores binding robustness (\ddg), and explains each mutation's effect with an LLM. Both outputs have ground truth, so we test both on HIV-1 protease (6 PIs) and reverse transcriptase (4 NNRTIs). \textbf{The docking score is beaten by its own geometry}: ranking mutations by distance to the ligand, same receptor but no docking run, tracks measured fold-resistance better than \ddg{} on both targets. The energy earns its cost only at the top of the ranking in the rigid NNRTI pocket, where each drug's top ten holds 7--8 real resistance mutations against 3--4 for distance; on the flexible protease site that ordering reverses. An LLM judge marks 71--72\% of the explanations as recovering the correct primary mechanism, and an \textbf{attribution ablation} over real, absent and corrupted structural context shows they condition on the pipeline, most strongly where the model's prior is weakest. A verification using no language model reproduces that ordering. Structural geometry carries the signal; the docking energy mostly does not.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Resistance is the central failure mode of antiviral therapy. A medicinal chemist with twenty candidate inhibitors must decide which few to advance into slow, expensive phenotypic resistance assays, a decision currently made by hand. A natural computational aid is to dock each candidate against a panel of known clinical resistance mutants and rank candidates by how well predicted binding \emph{survives} the panel.

Such a tool raises two questions that are usually asked separately, and that both have ground truth:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Does the prediction hold up?} Stanford HIVdb records \emph{measured} fold-resistance for essentially every approved HIV drug \citep{rhee2006}. This asks whether the score predicts a measured number, and whether it beats the trivial baselines a chemist could use instead.
\item \textbf{Does the \emph{explanation} hold up?} If the tool also explains \emph{why} a mutation threatens a compound, that explanation can be checked against mechanisms experts have already documented.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Contributions.} (i) An audit of structure-based resistance triage on two HIV targets, showing a docking-free geometric baseline outranks docking \ddg{} on both, with one exception at the top of the ranking in the rigid NNRTI pocket where the energy does earn its cost. (ii) Decision-relevant evaluation in place of a single pooled statistic: per-drug precision@$k$, significance-tested baselines, and a dropout sensitivity analysis. (iii) An attribution ablation for the pipeline's LLM explanations, transferring the corrupted-context logic of Min et al.~\citep{min2022} to structural input, with a verification that uses no language model. (iv) A reproducible target-agnostic implementation, with all docking results, explanations, ground truth and judge scores released.

\section{Related work}

\textbf{Rule-based expert systems} are the clinical standard and our strongest baseline. The Stanford HIVdb penalty algorithm \citep{liu2006,rhee2006} and the IAS-USA mutation lists \citep{iasusa} encode decades of genotype--phenotype and clinical-outcome evidence into per-mutation, per-drug scores. They are accurate because that evidence exists; they say nothing about a compound or target for which it does not.

\textbf{Supervised ML on genotype--phenotype data} is the dominant approach and reaches high retrospective accuracy, up to mean AUC $\approx0.97$ with protein language-model embeddings \citep{beerenwinkel2003,steiner2020,blassel2021,farquhar2026}. We do not claim to beat it. It carries one requirement that motivates our work: a labeled resistance dataset \emph{for the drug in question}. Docking is training-free, so it applies to a novel scaffold or a target with no resistance corpus. Whether that applicability is worth anything is what we test.

\textbf{Structure-based resistance prediction} has an established explanation for why protease is hard. The substrate-envelope hypothesis \citep{king2004,nalam2010} holds that resistance mutations disrupt inhibitor binding where the inhibitor protrudes beyond the consensus substrate volume, while leaving substrate processing intact. That is a coevolutionary, network-level constraint a single-structure pocket-affinity score is not designed to capture, and it predicts our protease result. Receptor flexibility is the standard remedy for rigid-docking failure \citep{amaro2008}; we do not employ it, which bounds our negative protease result.

\textbf{LLM explanation faithfulness.} Whether a model's stated reasoning reflects the computation producing its answer is distinct from whether the answer is right \citep{jacovi2020,turpin2023}. Our corrupted-context condition follows Min et al.~\citep{min2022}, who showed that randomizing in-context labels barely degrades performance, implying the model was not conditioning on them; we apply that test to structural context. LLM-as-judge scores are judge-dependent \citep{zheng2023}, so we rest our claims on paired within-judge contrasts. Our design is not new; its transfer to \emph{structural} input is, as is a verification that uses no language model at all. Appendix~\ref{app:related} covers the alternatives to rigid docking and the neighbouring perturb-and-rejudge work.

\section{Methods}
\textbf{Pipeline} (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}). A candidate SMILES is embedded and docked (AutoDock Vina \citep{vina}, or Uni-Dock \citep{unidock} on GPU) against a cleaned wildtype receptor and a per-drug panel of PDBFixer-built point-mutant receptors; the per-mutation \ddg{} (mutant minus wildtype binding) feeds a 0--100 robustness score. The Vina score is an empirical scoring function, not a binding free energy; we use it as a ranking signal only.

\textbf{Targets and data.} Protease (6 PIs, 1{,}591 scored pairs) and reverse transcriptase (4 NNRTIs, 1{,}269 pairs); structures, panel sizes and docking boxes in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}. We scope RT to NNRTIs (allosteric pocket binders): rigid docking is a sound proxy for NNRTI affinity but not for NRTI mechanisms. Measured fold-resistance and DRM labels come from Stanford HIVdb / IAS-USA \citep{rhee2006,iasusa}.

\textbf{Explanations and ground truth.} For each mutation we prompt an LLM (Claude Haiku 4.5) with a structural-context record (residue identity, $\Delta$volume/charge/hydrophobicity, ligand distance, direct-contact flag, subpocket) plus the docking \ddg, for a 2--3 sentence mechanistic hypothesis. Ground truth combines a curated set (IAS-USA $+$ primary literature) with an \emph{agentic} builder that researches each mechanism over live PubMed, cites the papers, and self-verifies grounding. All RT ground truth is agent-built and PR's is hand-curated, so we lead the faithfulness headline with PR.

\textbf{Faithfulness and attribution.} An LLM-as-judge scores each explanation against the ground-truth mechanism on a 0/1/2 rubric (judge robustness: \S\ref{sec:faith}). The attribution ablation regenerates explanations under \emph{full} (real context $+$ \ddg), \emph{minimal} (drug $+$ mutation only), and \emph{corrupted} (pipeline-derived fields deranged across pairs, intrinsic chemistry kept) conditions with the same model and judge; a field-level variant drops one context group at a time.

\textbf{Predictive benchmark.} Every claim carries a permutation $p$-value or bootstrap 95\% CI, scored against docking-free baselines: mutation prevalence, $|\Delta\text{volume}|$, ligand distance, and the Stanford HIVdb penalty. Test protocol, de-confounding, jackknife and the absence of multiplicity correction are in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

\section{Results}

\subsection{The predictive benchmark: geometry, not energy}
\label{sec:pred}
On both enzymes, rigid single-structure docking-\ddg{} is beaten by a baseline that uses the same receptor but never docks anything. The one exception tells us where the energy step is worth its cost.

\textbf{A docking-free geometric baseline beats \ddg{} globally, on both targets.} The field-level ablation of \S\ref{sec:abl} finds the \emph{explanations} carried by geometry rather than energy; so is the \emph{ranking}. Scoring each mutation by how close its residue lies to the ligand, with no docking run, raises the magnitude correlation with measured fold-resistance from $\rhoo=0.002$ to $0.387$ on protease, above HIVdb's 0.360, and DRM-recovery ROC-AUC from 0.51 [0.46, 0.56] to 0.963 [0.954, 0.971] (RT: 0.70 to 0.800 [0.738, 0.854]). We lead with the magnitude column because DRM labels are enriched for pocket-proximal residues almost by construction, which makes the ROC comparison partly definitional. The ordering is the same on both.

\textbf{The exception: at the top of the list, on RT, docking wins.} ROC-AUC scores a whole ranking, but the tool is read as the top few flagged mutations for one compound, and the two metrics disagree. Taking each drug's own top 10, docking recovers 7 to 8 genuine clinical DRMs per NNRTI against 3 to 4 for the distance baseline. On protease the result reverses, 0 to 3 for docking against 7 to 10 for distance (Fig.~\ref{fig:toplist}; Appendix~\ref{app:perdrug}). In the NNRTI pocket proximity is necessary but not sufficient, since many close residues are not resistance mutations, and discriminating \emph{within} the pocket is the one thing the energy buys. On protease it buys nothing at any depth of the list.

\textbf{Why the targets differ} (Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmark}, Appendix~\ref{app:benchmark})\textbf{.} On protease docking sits at chance (Spearman $\approx 0$; top-40 enrichment $2.86\times$, $p<0.001$, so tail enrichment coexists with chance-level global ranking) and de-confounding does not rescue it ($\rhoo \leq 0$). That is expected: protease resistance is co-evolving and substrate-envelope-constrained \citep{king2004,nalam2010}, violating the locality the method assumes (Appendix~\ref{app:failure}). On RT the same protocol is above chance (enrichment $8.35\times$) and de-confounding \emph{does} rescue the magnitude correlation, $-0.11 \to \rhoo=0.36$ [0.12, 0.56] ($p=6\times10^{-4}$) on single-mutation isolates.

\subsection{Explanation faithfulness}
\label{sec:faith}
Under the Claude-Haiku judge, explanations recover the correct primary mechanism 72\% of the time on protease ($n=46$, hand-curated ground truth) and 71\% on RT ($n=55$, agent-built ground truth, so its absolute value is the weaker of the two); most misses under-specify rather than contradict. The outright misses cluster on distal and inter-subunit DRMs, notably the E138--K101 salt bridge across the p51/p66 interface \citep{das2013}, where the structural context itself is blind rather than the model (Appendix~\ref{app:failure}).

\textbf{Judge robustness.} A second Claude model re-grading all 138 protease ablation items reproduces the \S\ref{sec:abl} contrasts, and GPT-5.6 agrees with the Haiku judge 95\% within one point but only 48\% exactly, Claude being systematically \emph{stricter}. We therefore treat the \emph{absolute} percentage as judge-dependent \citep{zheng2023} and rest our claims on the paired contrasts (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).

\textbf{An out-of-family check.} All three judges are LLMs, so the above establishes consistency, not correctness. Appendix~\ref{app:struct} adds a check involving no language model: whether an explanation's claim about ligand contact matches the receptor geometry. It reproduces the ablation ordering (88\% correct under full context, 57\% minimal, 55\% corrupted; Fisher $p=5.7\times10^{-5}$ full vs.\ corrupted) and shows the judge scoring structurally-wrong explanations lower (1.02 vs.\ 1.51, $p=6.1\times10^{-4}$). Both results most exposed to the circularity objection therefore survive a measurement taken outside the LLM family.

\subsection{Attribution ablation: the pipeline drives faithfulness where the prior is weak}
\label{sec:abl}

\textbf{Context is actually used.} Pooled across both targets ($n=101$), full context beats minimal (drug $+$ mutation only) by $\Delta=+0.27$ ($p=7\times10^{-4}$) and beats corrupted context by $\Delta=+0.46$ ($p<10^{-4}$). Feeding \emph{wrong} structural facts is worse than feeding none, so by the logic of Min et al.~\citep{min2022} the explanations are conditioning on the pipeline rather than pattern-matching the mutation name (Appendix~\ref{app:worked}). \emph{How much is the contact flag?} Deranging geometry \emph{within} contact class, leaving that flag true, recovers most of the loss (1.63 vs.\ 1.15 corrupted, 1.78 full), but real context still wins ($\Delta=+0.15$, $p=0.035$). The finer geometry contributes beyond the boolean, though the headline arm overstates it fourfold (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).

\textbf{Prior-strength dose-response.} The benefit of correct context scales inversely with the model's prior (Fig.~\ref{fig:interp}a). Where the prior alone is \emph{wrong} (minimal $=0$, $n=13$), context corrects the explanation in 11 of 13 cases (85\%); where vague (minimal $=1$), 66\%; where already correct (minimal $=2$), context is at ceiling. Because the raw gain is partly mechanical (a 0 has twice the headroom of a 1) we lead with these ceiling-robust rates; restricting to pairs with room to improve (minimal $<2$, $n=45$), context still helps $\Delta=+0.80$ ($p<10^{-4}$) and the gradient persists (Spearman $-0.40$, $p=0.006$).

\textbf{Which fields carry the lift.} A field-level ablation (protease, $n=46$; Fig.~\ref{fig:interp}b) drops one context group at a time. Removing the \emph{ligand distance} costs the most ($\Delta=+0.22$, $p=0.051$) and the \emph{subpocket} next ($\Delta=+0.15$, $p=0.09$); removing the \emph{docking \ddg} ($\Delta=-0.02$) or the physicochemistry ($\Delta=-0.02$) does nothing measurable. Marginally, and on one target ($n=46$), that points to geometric localization rather than energy. The \ddg{} null is absence of evidence, and specific to \emph{rigid, single-pose, waters-stripped Vina}: a better energy \citep{amaro2008,hauser2018} might contribute where ours does not.

\section{Discussion}
Both halves point the same way: \textbf{structural geometry carries the signal, and the docking energy mostly does not.} Ranking by ligand distance beats docking \ddg{} globally on both targets, and dropping distance or subpocket costs the most faithfulness while dropping \ddg{} costs nothing measurable. Two independent analyses reach one conclusion, a negative result about the method we built. The energy is not worthless. It discriminates \emph{within} the NNRTI pocket, where proximity alone cannot, and that is the whole of what we can show it buys. Structure still beats the non-structural shortcuts where resistance is structurally local; the \emph{energy} step is what fails to earn its cost. The baselines that beat docking need per-drug data a novel compound lacks (Appendix~\ref{app:access}), and nothing in the \ddg{} path is fitted to resistance labels. Both boundaries follow from the premise rather than surprising it, and so do the individual failures (Appendix~\ref{app:failure}): eight of nine trace to what the pipeline cannot represent rather than to the language model. We offer this from two contrasting HIV enzymes as a hypothesis, not a law: measure the geometry, treat the docking energy as unproven, and distrust structural triage for flexible, co-evolving sites.

\textbf{Limitations.} Docking-energy claims are bounded by this protocol: rigid single-structure Vina, one pose, waters stripped, and 7.1\% of RT dockings dropped for returning no pose. The faithfulness half rests on $n=46$ and $n=55$ pairs over two enzymes, with load-bearing contrasts marginal ($p=0.035$, $0.051$, $0.09$) and no multiplicity correction. RT ground truth is agent-built, all three judges are language models, and most of the corruption effect is a single contact flag (\S\ref{sec:abl}). Appendix~\ref{app:repro} details each.

\textbf{Future work.} A \emph{human-expert anchor}, 20 to 30 explanations graded blind on the same rubric by a structural biologist, is the one measurement that would move the faithfulness result from consistent to correct; the sample and primer are released. \emph{More targets} would test geometry-over-energy beyond two HIV enzymes; neuraminidase and SARS-CoV-2 M\textsuperscript{pro} are already wired in. \emph{A better energy} (ensemble docking \citep{amaro2008}, free-energy calculation \citep{hauser2018}) would separate a null about docking from a null about this protocol. \emph{Replication at larger $n$}, including on RT, would settle the marginal decomposition claims ($p=0.035$ to $0.09$ at $n=46$); every number here is also retrospective (\S\ref{sec:pred}).

\section{Per-drug breakdown and precision@\texorpdfstring{$k$}{k}}
\label{app:perdrug}

Table~\ref{tab:perdrug} gives the per-drug metrics behind the pooled numbers in
\S4.1, and Table~\ref{tab:patk} the decision-relevant precision@$k$. Both are
produced by \texttt{scripts/14\_precision\_at\_k.py}.

Precision@$k$ ranks a \emph{single} drug's own mutation panel by predicted \ddg{}
(descending, matching the pooled enrichment convention) and reports how many of
the top $k$ are known major DRMs. Significance is an exact one-sided
hypergeometric test, giving the probability that drawing $k$ mutations at random
from that drug's panel yields at least as many DRMs. That is appropriate at $k=5$
where a bootstrap CI is uninformative.

The per-drug view shows the pooled result is not an artifact of averaging. Every
NNRTI clears its base rate by a wide margin at every $k$; among the PIs only
atazanavir ($k=5$) and lopinavir ($k=20$) reach $p<0.05$, and indinavir, whose
ROC-AUC of 0.388 is \emph{below} chance, returns no DRMs in its top 10. The
protease failure is therefore not uniform noise: it is one target on which the
method is, for some drugs, actively anti-correlated with the truth.

The last column is the comparison \S\ref{sec:pred} rests on, and it runs opposite
to the ROC-AUC ordering of Table~\ref{tab:geom}. Distance is the better global
ranker on both targets, but at $k=10$ it wins only on protease (7 to 10 of 10,
against 0 to 3 for docking) and loses on every NNRTI (3 to 4, against 7 to 8).
The two metrics are measuring different things: distance orders the whole list
well because DRMs are pocket-proximal, and then fills its top ten with residues
that are close but not resistance mutations. Discriminating inside the pocket is
what the energy contributes, and it contributes it only where the pocket is
rigid.

Table~\ref{tab:geom} is the sharpest negative result in the paper: as a global
ranker, the single number ``how far is this residue from the ligand'' outranks a
full docking sweep on both targets, and on protease it also beats the expert
system on the magnitude column. (At the top of the list the ordering reverses on
RT; see Table~\ref{tab:patk}.) Two readings are available and we do not think the evidence separates
them. The deflationary one is that DRM status for a competitive inhibitor is
close to a statement about pocket proximity, so a distance predictor is partly
recovering the definition of the label; this is why we lead with the magnitude
correlation against \emph{measured} fold-resistance, where the same ordering
holds but the margin is smaller. The constructive one is that the geometric
localization of a mutation, which docking computes en route to its energy, is
where the usable signal lives, and the scoring step discards rather than adds.
The same conclusion arrives independently from the field-level ablation on the
explanation side (\S\ref{sec:abl}), which is what makes us take it seriously.

single compound. Both are produced by \texttt{scripts/12\_baselines.py}.

\section{Worked example: one mutation through the ablation}

\textbf{Full context} (score 2). \emph{``The M46I mutation increases

contacting it\ldots{} reflects reduced entropic penalty for flap closure and

\textbf{Minimal context} (score 0). \emph{``The M46I substitution replaces a
larger, flexible methionine with a smaller isoleucine, creating a structural
cavity in the S1 subpocket where indinavir's P1 ligand group normally makes
critical van der Waals contacts\ldots{}''} With only the drug and mutation name,
the model defaults to an active-site packing story and \emph{contradicts} the
documented mechanism.

\textbf{Corrupted context} (score 0). \emph{``\ldots{}isoleucine's shorter
branched aliphatic chain cannot maintain the same contact surface\ldots{}the
$\sim$4\,\AA{} minimum distance suggests the residue is in direct contact with the
ligand\ldots{}destabilizes the inhibitor by approximately 0.66\kcal, particularly
affecting the packing interactions critical for IDV's potency in this
region.''}

The corrupted output is the informative one: the explanation \emph{quotes the
falsified values back} (the 4\,\AA{} distance, the S2/S2$'$ region, the 0.66\kcal{}
energy) and reaches the wrong mechanism because of them. This is direct evidence
that the generated mechanism is conditioned on the pipeline's structural fields
rather than retrieved from the model's prior on a famous mutation name, and it is
the single-instance version of the aggregate result in \S4.3.

\textbf{Judge rationales.} The judge returns a one-sentence justification with
every score; for this pair they are, verbatim:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{full} (2): ``The explanation correctly identifies M46I as a flap
mutation that operates through conformational dynamics rather than direct
contact, accurately describing how altered hydrophobic landscape and flap
closure dynamics indirectly weaken inhibitor stabilization, the core known
mechanism.''
\item \emph{minimal} (0): ``The explanation incorrectly claims M46I creates a
structural cavity that disrupts direct van der Waals contacts with indinavir's
P1 ligand group, contradicting the known mechanism that Met46 is a flap residue
that does NOT contact the inhibitor directly\ldots''
\item \emph{corrupted} (0): ``The explanation contradicts the known mechanism by
claiming M46I reduces binding through direct van der Waals contacts with
indinavir, when the ground truth explicitly states Met46 is a flap residue that
does not contact the inhibitor directly\ldots''
\end{itemize}

Both failures turn on the same discriminating fact, whether residue 46 contacts
the ligand, which is exactly the field the corruption flips
(\texttt{contacts\_ligand\_directly}: \texttt{False} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{True})
and exactly the field group the field-level ablation identifies as most load-bearing
(\S4.3, \emph{drop\_distance}). The same pair appears in all four PIs that carry
M46I (ATV, IDV, LPV, NFV) with the identical 2/0/0 score pattern, so it is a
reproducible instance rather than a single sampled outlier.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{app:repro}

\textbf{Structures and docking.} Protease: PDB 3OXC, chains A/B, catalytic Asp25
dyad asymmetrically protonated, mutations applied to both chains of the homodimer;
box centre $(5.341, -1.893, 14.179)$, size $22^3$\,\AA. RT: PDB 3V81, p66/p51
heterodimer, NNRTI pocket; box centre $(41.105, 52.332, 49.098)$ (occupancy-weighted
centroid of the chain-A nevirapine ligand; note that 3V81 contains a second NVP copy
whose inclusion moves the centroid into empty space), size $24^3$\,\AA; wildtype
absolute affinities of $-9$ to $-12$\kcal{} confirm the box is on the pocket.
AutoDock Vina 1.2 (CPU) / Uni-Dock (GPU), \texttt{exhaustiveness} 16, 5 poses
retained per run, best pose scored. Receptors cleaned with PDBFixer; mutants built by point
mutagenesis with a frozen-environment clash-relief minimization of the mutated
side chain only; ligands prepared with RDKit (\texttt{AddHs}, ETKDG) and meeko.
Crystal waters, including the conserved flap water, are stripped.

\textbf{Docking-dropout sensitivity.} Pairs whose docking returned no valid pose
are excluded, which would bias the benchmark if those pairs were DRM-enriched.
They are not: \textbf{zero} of the 97 excluded RT pairs and zero of the 6 excluded
protease pairs are major DRMs, against a 9.0\% DRM rate among the 1{,}269 scored
RT pairs (Fisher exact $p=3.6\times10^{-4}$). All 30 RT and all 33 protease
primary DRMs survive into the scored set. The residual worry is the reverse: the
excluded pairs are non-DRMs that, had they docked, might have scored high and
acted as false positives. We bound that directly by imputing every excluded pair
at the \emph{worst case} (a \ddg{} above every scored pair, i.e.\ ranked top and
all wrong): RT's pooled ROC-AUC falls only from 0.695 to \textbf{0.641}, still
above chance, and protease is unchanged (0.510 to 0.508). Median imputation moves
neither target. Produced by \texttt{scripts/15\_geometry\_baseline.py}.

\textbf{Scale, yield and cost.} Protease: 1{,}597 drug--mutation dockings
attempted across 6 PIs, of which 6 (0.4\%) returned no valid pose, leaving
\textbf{1{,}591 scored pairs over 285 mutant receptors}. RT: 1{,}366 attempted
across 4 NNRTIs, of which 97 (7.1\%) returned no valid pose, leaving
\textbf{1{,}269 scored pairs over 423 mutant receptors}. Pairs without a valid
pose are excluded from every analysis rather than imputed; the RT failure rate is
an order of magnitude higher than protease's and is itself a caveat on the RT
result, since the excluded set is not guaranteed to be missing at random. All 30
RT primary DRMs and all 33 protease primary DRMs are present in the scored set.
The full RT sweep took 19 minutes on a single A100 80GB with Uni-Dock. At roughly
2.7\,s per receptor this is orders of magnitude cheaper per mutation than an
alchemical free-energy protocol such as Hauser et al.~\citep{hauser2018} (which we
did not benchmark directly), and that cost/accuracy trade is the one the paper is
about.

\textbf{Judge robustness (detail for \S\ref{sec:faith}).} On a 21-item sample
stratified for the judge's hard (0/1) cases, GPT-5.6 agrees with Claude-Haiku
95\% within one point but 48\% exactly ($\kappa$ 0.23/0.38 unweighted/linear);
Claude is systematically stricter. Re-grading all 138 protease ablation items
with a second Claude model (Sonnet 5) reproduces the \S\ref{sec:abl} contrasts:
full$-$minimal $+0.35$ and full$-$corrupted $+0.65$, against Haiku's $+0.43$ and
$+0.63$ ($\kappa=0.64/0.72$ unweighted/linear). The paired contrasts survive the
change of judge; the absolute percentage does not.

\textbf{Models.} Generation and judging both use \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}
(\texttt{max\_tokens} 1024 for generation, 512 for judging); larger Claude models
decline HIV-resistance \emph{generation} but not judging, which is why the second
judge (\texttt{claude-sonnet-5}) was available for the cross-model check. Scores
are returned through a forced tool call against a fixed \texttt{report\_score}
schema, so every verdict is machine-readable and no free-text parsing is involved.

\textbf{Generation prompt} (verbatim system prompt; the enzyme noun and binding
site are substituted per target, either ``HIV-1 protease''/``active site'' or ``HIV-1
reverse transcriptase''/``allosteric NNRTI-binding pocket''): \emph{``You are a
structural biologist specializing in HIV-1 protease drug resistance. Given
structural context about a point mutation and its effect on inhibitor binding,
provide a 2-3 sentence mechanistic hypothesis for WHY this mutation causes
resistance to this specific drug. Ground your explanation in the structural facts
provided (residue size/charge change, distance to the active site, subpocket). Be
specific about molecular interactions (van der Waals contacts, hydrogen bonds,
steric clashes, electrostatic changes). Do not speculate beyond the given data,
and do not restate the numbers back --- interpret them. No preamble.''}

The user message is templated as
\begin{quote}\footnotesize\ttfamily
Drug: \{drug\_full\} (\{drug\}), a \{drug\_class\}\\
Mutation: \{mutation\}\\
Predicted delta-delta-G: \{ddg\} kcal/mol (positive = weaker binding = resistance)\\
Structural context:\\
\{context JSON, indent 2\}\\
\\
Explain the structural mechanism by which \{mutation\} likely reduces \{drug\_full\} binding.
\end{quote}
The \emph{minimal} condition drops the \ddg{} line and the context JSON entirely,
keeping the drug and mutation lines and the closing instruction; \emph{full} and
\emph{corrupted} reproduce the production prompt verbatim (with \texttt{cite=False}),
so the ablation's control arm is the real pipeline rather than a re-implementation.

\textbf{Corruption procedure.} The corrupted condition replaces six
pipeline-derived fields with those of another pair:
\texttt{distance\_from\_ligand\_centroid\_angstrom},
\texttt{min\_distance\_to\_ligand\_angstrom}, \texttt{contacts\_ligand\_directly},
\texttt{region}, \texttt{delta\_delta\_g} and \texttt{delta\_g} (the \ddg{} shown
in the prompt follows the donor). Donors are assigned by a \emph{derangement}
(sampled with seed 0, rejecting any permutation with a fixed point), so no pair
keeps its own values and every corrupted context is realistic-but-wrong.
Intrinsic-chemistry fields (position, wildtype and mutant residue identity, and
the volume/charge/hydrophobicity changes) are deliberately \emph{not} corrupted,
because the mutation string itself determines them and scrambling them would make
the context self-contradictory rather than merely false. A consequence worth
stating: on low-cardinality fields such as \texttt{region} a derangement can
coincide with the true value by chance, so the corruption is a lower bound on the
effect a directed (antipodal) corruption would produce.

\textbf{Contact-preserving corruption (control).} Because
\texttt{contacts\_ligand\_directly} is close to the answer for a
mechanism-classification question, we ran a stricter variant that isolates the
rest of the geometry. Donors are drawn \emph{within contact class}, so contacting
residues donate only to contacting residues, and the flag is restored to its
true value afterwards, so it is both correct and consistent with the donated
distance (no self-contradictory prompt). The derangement is over distinct
\emph{mutations} rather than (drug, mutation) pairs, since the same mutation
recurs across drugs with identical geometry; the contact flag is preserved on all
46 protease pairs and the distance genuinely changes on 41 of them.

Faithfulness under this condition is 1.630 (72\% correct), against 1.152 (41\%)
for the full derangement and 1.783 (80\%) for real context. Three paired
Wilcoxon contrasts follow: contact-preserved beats full corruption by
$\Delta=+0.478$ ($p=5.8\times10^{-4}$), while real context still beats
contact-preserved by $\Delta=+0.152$ ($p=0.035$; 9 up, 3 down, 34 tied). The
decomposition is therefore about 76\% of the corruption effect attributable to
the contact boolean and about 24\% to distance, subpocket and energy. We report
this because it materially qualifies \S\ref{sec:abl}: the pipeline does
contribute beyond a single flag, but the headline \emph{corrupted} arm overstates
that contribution by roughly a factor of four. Produced by
\texttt{scripts/17\_contact\_preserving\_corruption.py}.

\textbf{Field-level ablation groups.} Each condition strips one group from the
context: \emph{drop\_\ddg} removes \texttt{delta\_delta\_g} and \texttt{delta\_g}
and nulls the \ddg{} prompt line; \emph{drop\_distance} removes
\texttt{distance\_from\_ligand\_centroid\_angstrom},
\texttt{min\_distance\_to\_ligand\_angstrom} and
\texttt{contacts\_ligand\_directly}; \emph{drop\_subpocket} removes
\texttt{region}; \emph{drop\_chem} removes \texttt{volume\_change},
\texttt{volume\_change\_A3}, \texttt{charge\_change} and
\texttt{hydrophobicity\_change}. All other fields and the prompt are unchanged.

\textbf{Judge prompt} (verbatim system prompt): \emph{``You are evaluating whether
an AI-generated explanation of HIV-1 protease drug resistance correctly identifies
the known structural mechanism. Score 0 if the explanation contradicts the known
mechanism, 1 if it is consistent but vague or misses the key interaction, 2 if it
correctly identifies the primary structural basis. Judge only structural/mechanistic
agreement, not writing style, and give a one-sentence justification.''} The enzyme
noun is substituted per target. The judge sees the mutation, the drug, the
ground-truth mechanism and the explanation. It never sees the condition label, the
\ddg, or which system produced the explanation.

\textbf{Statistics.} Permutation tests use 5{,}000 label shuffles and bootstrap
CIs 5{,}000 resamples, seed 0. Enrichment ranks by \ddg{} descending. Paired
ablation contrasts use the one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test at the
(drug, mutation) level, which is the appropriate paired non-parametric test for
the bounded, tied, non-Gaussian 0/1/2 score differences we observe; a paired
$t$-test would assume a normality the difference distribution violates.
Precision@$k$ significance is the exact hypergeometric test described in
Appendix~\ref{app:perdrug}. We report many tests without multiplicity correction;
the headline contrasts survive at $p<10^{-3}$.

\textbf{Ground truth.} Protease mechanisms are hand-curated from the IAS-USA 2022
mutation list and the primary structural literature, with the source recorded per
entry. RT mechanisms (22 entries) are built by a tool-using agent that queries
live PubMed for each mutation, cites the papers it read, and self-verifies that
the stated mechanism is supported before the entry is admitted; entries failing
that check are dropped rather than included unverified. Because all RT ground
truth is agent-built while all protease ground truth is human-curated, absolute
faithfulness percentages are not strictly comparable \emph{across} targets. The
within-target ablation contrasts, which hold ground truth fixed, are.

\textbf{Software.} Python 3.11; pandas 2.2.3, numpy 1.26.4, scipy 1.15.3,
scikit-learn 1.7.0, anthropic 0.74.0; RDKit, OpenMM, PDBFixer, AutoDock Vina and
meeko via conda (\texttt{environment.yml}). The pipeline is reproduced by
\texttt{scripts/01}--\texttt{14} in order; each number in this paper is produced by
a named script, and the mapping is in the repository README.

\textbf{Data availability.} All docking results, explanations under every ablation
condition, ground-truth mechanisms, judge scores with justifications, and the
derived tables in this appendix are released with the code, so every figure and
table here can be regenerated without re-running docking or re-querying any model.

\section{Failure-mode analysis}
\label{app:failure}

The method makes one implicit assumption: \emph{that resistance is a local,
contact-mediated perturbation of ligand binding, visible in a single rigid
structure.} Every failure we observe violates one clause of that sentence, and
which clause it violates predicts the failure. Table~\ref{tab:failure} organises
them; the point is that these are not scattered noise but four identifiable
regimes, three of which are properties of the \emph{pipeline}, not of the model.

& contact-mediated \& single-subunit
& The true mechanism is the E138--K101 salt bridge across the p51/p66 interface \citep{das2013}. The pipeline reports \texttt{min\_distance\_to\_ligand} $=43.0$\,\AA{} (measured to the chain-A ligand) and \texttt{contacts\_ligand\_directly} $=$ False, so the model reasons \emph{correctly} from a numerically true fact to the wrong conclusion, ``$\sim$43\,\AA{} away, negligible impact.'' The context has no inter-subunit coupling feature. \\
\addlinespace
A98G (RT)
& none (model error)
& The context is correct and sufficient; the model simply dismisses a real accessory contribution as ``essentially neutral.'' The only miss of the nine attributable to the model rather than the pipeline. \\
\addlinespace
L100I (RT)
& single rigid pose
& The model attributes resistance to a change of binding mode; a single-pose rigid protocol cannot represent one. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Two consequences worth stating plainly. First, eight of the nine faithfulness
misses are attributable to what the pipeline could not represent rather than to
the language model; only A98G is a plain model error. The E138 cluster is the
clearest case: the LLM was faithful to its input and the input was
mechanistically misleading. This is the sharpest illustration
of the paper's own thesis, since geometric context is what carries the explanation,
so wrong or incomplete geometry is what breaks it. It also names a concrete
fix (an inter-subunit contact feature) rather than a vague one. Second, the
protease and RT failures are complementary: protease fails because resistance is
non-local, RT's residual failures because the locality \emph{measure} is
incomplete. Neither is repaired by a better language model.

This analysis is also the one part of the faithfulness evaluation that does not
depend on an LLM. Each diagnosis above is checkable against artifacts outside the
loop: that E138 sits 43.0\,\AA{} from the chain-A ligand is a fact of the
structure file, and that Met46 and Leu90 are non-contact residues is recorded in
the IAS-USA list. It is a partial check only, validating \emph{why} the misses
occur, not the absolute faithfulness percentage, but it does establish that the
score-0 cases are real failures with identifiable causes rather than judge
artifacts.

\section{An LLM-free structural-consistency check}
\label{app:struct}

Every check in \S\ref{sec:faith} is internal to a family of language models. This
appendix adds one that is not, by exploiting the fact that a single claim in each
explanation is mechanically verifiable against the receptor: \emph{does the mutated
residue touch the ligand?}

\textbf{Method.} For each of the 303 explanations (46 protease and 55 RT pairs
$\times$ 3 conditions) we take the true minimum residue--ligand distance from the
structure (contact iff $<4$\,\AA) and, separately, classify what the explanation
\emph{asserts} using a fixed regex lexicon in which negation is resolved first, so
that ``without directly contacting it'' is scored as a non-contact claim rather
than a contact one. Explanations making no explicit claim \emph{abstain} and are
excluded from accuracy rather than counted wrong. No language model participates
in the measurement; the lexicon and the per-explanation calls are released with
the code (\texttt{scripts/16\_structural\_consistency.py}).

\textbf{Coverage.} 139 of 303 explanations (46\%) make an explicit contact claim.
Abstention is itself condition-dependent: stripped of the distance field, the
\emph{minimal} arm makes far fewer checkable claims ($n=14$), which is why that
cell is the weakest of the three.

\textbf{Result A: the ablation reproduces without an LLM} (Table~\ref{tab:struct}).
Explanations written with the real structural context get the contact question
right 88\% of the time; stripped of it, 57\%; given deranged geometry, 55\%
(Fisher exact $p=0.013$ full vs.\ minimal, $p=5.7\times10^{-5}$ full vs.\
corrupted). The ordering, the magnitude and the near-equality of the minimal and
corrupted arms all match the judge-scored result of \S\ref{sec:abl}, obtained by
an entirely different route.

\textbf{Result B: the judge tracks an external criterion.} Explanations that are
structurally \emph{wrong} by this test receive a mean judge score of 1.02, versus
1.51 for structurally correct ones (Mann--Whitney one-sided $p=6.1\times10^{-4}$;
$n=42$ and 97). The LLM judge is therefore not merely agreeing with other LLMs:
its scores move with a criterion computed from a PDB file.

\centering
\caption{\textbf{Contact-claim accuracy by condition}, computed from receptor geometry with no language model. Accuracy is over explanations making an explicit claim; abstentions are excluded.}
\label{tab:struct}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
condition & $n$ claims & accuracy & protease / RT \\
\midrule

\textbf{What this does and does not establish.} It is a check on one axis, over
the 46\% of explanations that commit to it, using a lexicon we wrote; it is not a
substitute for expert grading of the full mechanistic claim, and we do not report
it as one. What it does establish is that the two results most exposed to the
circularity objection, the ablation contrast and the judge's discrimination,
both survive a measurement taken outside the LLM family.

\section{Extended related work}
\label{app:related}

\textbf{Supervised ML on genotype--phenotype data.} \texttt{geno2pheno} learns
decision trees and SVMs mapping sequence to phenotypic resistance
\citep{beerenwinkel2003}; deep architectures (MLP, bidirectional RNN, CNN) have
been trained across 18 antiretrovirals \citep{steiner2020}; large-scale gradient
boosting and random forests over clinical sequence corpora recover both known and
novel resistance mutations \citep{blassel2021}; and protein language-model
embeddings push mean AUC to $\approx0.97$ \citep{farquhar2026}. Each needs a
labeled resistance dataset for the drug in question, which is the requirement
docking does not carry.

\textbf{Alternatives to rigid docking.} Other structure-aware approaches include
molecular field mapping over homology models \citep{ota2021} and, at the
accurate-but-expensive end, alchemical free-energy calculations (88\% accuracy,
$\approx1.1$\kcal{} RMSE on 144 clinical Abl mutations \citep{hauser2018}) at
orders of magnitude more compute per mutation than the 2.7\,s per receptor
reported above. Learned co-folding models are a fast-moving alternative
\citep{boltz2}, though benchmarking shows deep docking can produce physically
invalid poses and generalize poorly off-distribution \citep{posebusters}.

\textbf{Perturb-and-rejudge.} Perturbing the input and re-scoring with an LLM
judge is an established family: Porbeck et al.~\citep{porbeck2026} vary debugging
context and judge the resulting failure explanations, and Afolabi et
al.~\citep{afolabi2026} use causal ablation to test whether stated medical
reasoning drives the answer. Our design is not new; its transfer to
\emph{structural} input is, as is the verification of
Appendix~\ref{app:struct}, since one claim in every explanation here is
checkable against a PDB file with no language model. LLMs are increasingly
deployed as tool-using agents in chemistry \citep{chemcrow}, but the
faithfulness of their mechanistic claims in this domain is rarely measured.

\label{app:access}

Table~\ref{tab:baselineci} shows HIVdb beating docking on both targets. The
argument for running docking anyway rests on \emph{what data each predictor
requires}, which Table~\ref{tab:access} makes explicit rather than asserting.

Prevalence needs a corpus of clinical isolates genotyped \emph{and} tested against
that drug; HIVdb needs, in addition, expert curation of per-mutation per-drug
penalties distilled from accumulated genotype--phenotype and outcome data;
supervised models need labelled phenotypes for the drug they predict. None of
these exist for a compound that has never been dosed in a patient. Docking needs
only a receptor structure and the candidate's chemistry.

\textbf{What ``prospective'' does and does not mean here.} Our \ddg{} comes from a
physics/empirical docking engine (AutoDock Vina, or Uni-Dock's GPU
reimplementation of the same scoring function), not a learned one. This matters
for how the result should be evaluated. Deep docking and co-folding models
\citep{boltz2} carry billions of parameters trained on experimental complexes, so
a held-out test set is mandatory to show they learned structural biology rather
than memorizing training proteins, which is exactly the generalization gap
Buttenschoen et al.~\citep{posebusters} document. Vina is not parameter-free either: its handful of
scoring-term weights were fit once by regression on general protein--ligand
crystal data. But that fit involved no resistance phenotype, no HIVdb record and
nothing in this evaluation. The precise claim is therefore not ``nothing is
fit'' but \emph{nothing in the \ddg{} path is fit to resistance data}, which is
what rules out leakage into this benchmark. Consequently there is no
resistance-specific training set to hold out, and the per-drug numbers in
Table~\ref{tab:perdrug} are not a proxy for prospective performance. For a new
compound against these targets, they \emph{are} it.

Two boundaries on that. First, the \emph{mutation panel} is target-derived from
clinical data: docking tells you how a compound fares against a mutation, not
which mutations to test. So the method is prospective per compound but not yet
per target, and validating a genuinely novel target needs a resistance dataset
that, by construction, a novel target lacks. Second, the pipeline's one heavily
trained component is the LLM, for which the memorisation concern is real and
live; the \emph{minimal} condition of \S\ref{sec:abl} is precisely its control,
measuring what the model produces from the drug and mutation name alone.

\textbf{The honest qualification.} Being training-free is not by itself the
virtue: $|\Delta$volume$|$ is also computable for any novel compound, needing
nothing but the mutation string, and on RT it reaches ROC-AUC 0.653
[0.603, 0.701] against docking's 0.695 [0.625, 0.768], intervals that overlap
substantially (Table~\ref{tab:baselineci}). On protease it is 0.489, at chance,
as docking is. And the ligand-distance baseline of Table~\ref{tab:geom}, also structure-only
and also training-free, outranks docking globally on both targets. So the
defensible claim is narrower than ``docking is the only prospective option'':
among prospectively-available predictors, docking is the best we measured only at
the top of the ranking and only on RT (Table~\ref{tab:patk}), and on the global
metric it is not separated from a zero-cost physicochemical proxy. Separating
them, and testing transfer to a new target, are the experiments that would
complete the argument.
